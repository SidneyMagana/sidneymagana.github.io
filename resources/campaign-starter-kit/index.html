01 / Creative brief

Name the decision.

Choose one objective you can influence. A useful brief makes the work and the approval criteria clear.

Offer / service: Audience: Audience need and supporting evidence: Campaign objective: Core message: Verified facts and proof: Primary call to action and destination: Channel / format: Required assets and permissions: Constraints / deadline: Approver: How we will assess the work:

02 / Audience

Know what you know.

Customer input, published research, and assumptions are different kinds of information. Label them.

What I know about this audience: Source of that information: What I am assuming: Questions I still need to ask: Their likely hesitation: The language they actually use, if verified: One way to test the message:

03 / Message builder

Make the value specific.

For [audience] who need [specific help], [offer] provides [credible value]. Unlike [relevant alternative, if verified], the approach is [real distinction]. Support this with [verified proof]. Next step: [one clear action].

04 / Guided AI prompts

Ask better questions.

Use public or approved information. Remove private or confidential details before using an external tool. Check every output against your brief and evidence.

Audience hypotheses

Using only [verified audience information], suggest three possible needs or hesitations for [audience] considering [offer]. Separate evidence from hypotheses. Ask for missing information. Do not invent interviews or customer quotes.

Message options

Develop three message options for [offer] addressing [need]. Use these verified facts: [facts]. Include one value statement and one next step per option. Do not invent outcomes, testimonials, credentials, or product claims.

Campaign concepts

Suggest three campaign concepts for [audience], [offer], and [objective], on [channel]. For each, provide a hook, useful takeaway, format, and CTA. Stay within [resources and constraints]. Explain the tradeoffs.

Visual direction

Propose two visual directions for [approved message], using [brand guidelines]. Describe composition, typography, imagery, and accessible text treatment. Identify any required assets or licenses. Do not imply unconfirmed brand partnerships.

Critical review

Review this draft [content] against [brief and verified facts]. Flag unsupported claims, unclear wording, accessibility issues, and mismatched CTAs. Explain proposed edits. Do not replace missing evidence with invented details.

05 / Seven-day planner

A manageable week.

Three suggested publishing slots, with time to prepare and review. Adapt the cadence to your actual capacity.

DayFocusAction
Day 1ClarifyComplete the brief and identify missing facts.
Day 2ObserveReview audience information and check assumptions.
Day 3Publish 1Share a useful explanation connected to the audience’s need.
Day 4CreateDevelop the next visual and message; review readability.
Day 5Publish 2Show the offer or process with verified supporting detail.
Day 6ReviewCheck responses and questions. Avoid conclusions from tiny samples.
Day 7Publish 3Answer a relevant question and invite one clear next step.

06 / Fictional worked example

A clear invitation to begin.

Illustrative example only. This business, offer, and proposed content are fictional. No results are claimed.

Business: Willow Studio, a fictional wellness business.

Offer: A beginner introduction session.

Audience hypothesis: Adults curious about joining but unsure what a first visit involves.

Objective: Help visitors understand the session and find the inquiry page.

Message: Know what to expect before your first visit.

Content: A simple first-visit guide; a walkthrough of the session format; answers to common questions supplied and approved by the studio.

CTA: Read the session details and ask a question.

Verification needed: Actual schedule, accessibility information, price, session details, and any health-related claims.
