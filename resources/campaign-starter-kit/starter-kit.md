## Message builder
For [audience] who need [specific help], [offer] provides [credible value].
Unlike [relevant alternative, if verified], the approach is [real distinction].
Support this with [verified proof].
Next step: [one clear action].

## Five guided prompts

### Audience hypotheses
Using only [verified audience information], suggest three possible needs or hesitations for [audience] considering [offer]. Separate evidence from hypotheses. Ask for missing information. Do not invent interviews or customer quotes.

### Message options
Develop three message options for [offer] addressing [need]. Use these verified facts: [facts]. Include one value statement and one next step per option. Do not invent outcomes, testimonials, credentials, or product claims.

### Campaign concepts
Suggest three campaign concepts for [audience], [offer], and [objective], on [channel]. For each, provide a hook, useful takeaway, format, and CTA. Stay within [resources and constraints]. Explain the tradeoffs.

### Visual direction
Propose two visual directions for [approved message], using [brand guidelines]. Describe composition, typography, imagery, and accessible text treatment. Identify any required assets or licenses. Do not imply unconfirmed brand partnerships.

### Critical review
Review this draft [content] against [brief and verified facts]. Flag unsupported claims, unclear wording, accessibility issues, and mismatched CTAs. Explain proposed edits. Do not replace missing evidence with invented details.

## Seven-day planner
- Day 1 / Clarify: Complete the brief and identify missing facts.
- Day 2 / Observe: Review audience information and check assumptions.
- Day 3 / Publish 1: Share a useful explanation connected to the audience’s need.
- Day 4 / Create: Develop the next visual and message; review readability.
- Day 5 / Publish 2: Show the offer or process with verified supporting detail.
- Day 6 / Review: Check responses and questions. Avoid conclusions from tiny samples.
- Day 7 / Publish 3: Answer a relevant question and invite one clear next step.

## Fictional example
Willow Studio is a fictional wellness business introducing a beginner session. Audience hypothesis: curious adults unsure what a first visit involves. Message: Know what to expect before your first visit. Content: first-visit guide, session walkthrough, and studio-approved answers. CTA: Read the details and ask a question. Verify schedule, price, accessibility, session details, and claims before publishing. No results are claimed.

## Final checklist
- [ ] The audience and problem are clear.
- [ ] The content supports one offer or useful idea.
- [ ] Claims have evidence or client approval.
- [ ] The CTA destination is correct and works.
- [ ] Text is legible; images have suitable alt text.
- [ ] Sources, licenses, and permissions are checked.
- [ ] AI-assisted output has received human review.
