About Sidney

I like the moment
when something clicks.

I’m Sidney Magaña, a Bay Area AI Creative Strategist, freelance content creator, and human-centered digital storyteller. I connect people, ideas, and information—and turn that understanding into organized action.

Sometimes that means finding the cause of a technical problem. Sometimes it means giving a student a different explanation. And sometimes it means turning an idea into a world someone can explore.

I studied anthropology and Latin American studies because I was curious about how people make meaning. My cross-cultural research and published reporting in Rio de Janeiro taught me to listen closely to how people describe their experiences.

Supporting Google’s retail partners taught me to follow a problem through the data, technology, and teams responsible for fixing it. Amazon strengthened my ability to organize operations. Housing and property management keep me attentive to the details that affect people’s everyday lives.

I help e-commerce and marketing teams turn complex products and processes into clear content, practical workflows, and digital experiences people can use. My teaching experience brings another useful habit: when an explanation does not land, I try a different way in.

I’m outgoing, quick to start a conversation, and comfortable asking the basic question. I take responsibility seriously, and I make room for people to ask questions, learn, and enjoy working together.

For years, I did not think of myself as an artist. Generative AI and worldbuilding gave me another way to explore ideas. Through Adobe Digital Academy’s AI Creative Strategist program at General Assembly, I bring that curiosity into visual storytelling and creative production.

I work across English, Spanish, and Portuguese. Travel keeps me curious about people and places; my projects give that curiosity somewhere to go.

Teaching & mentor coordination

Sometimes the way in
is a pizza.

A learner was struggling with cloud computing. More terminology wasn’t helping. A familiar analogy did.

An explanation that clicked

At Mentor Me Collective, I used the established pizza-as-a-service analogy to explain how responsibilities shift between cloud service models. Who makes it? Who provides the equipment? Who handles which part?

Once the idea clicked, I noticed the learner moved through his quiz more quickly and became more responsive in class. My contribution was recognizing that he needed another route into the concept.

A firsthand teaching observation about one learner. I adapted an existing analogy; I did not invent it.

Clarity for the whole section

I supported approximately 100 learners preparing for the Google Cybersecurity Certificate and mentored 13 more closely. The program served people in Africa working toward opportunities in technology.

I created organizational documents and a Google Sheets dashboard for quick links, attendance, scheduling, grading, presentations, and mentor coordination. I also maintained learner departure records and helped mentors understand AI tools.

The goal was practical: less searching for direction, and a clearer place to find what happened next.
