The challenge

For lower-income households, a vehicle purchase could depend on months of credit improvement, careful budgeting, understanding financing terms and finding a suitable car. Clean-vehicle incentives added another layer of paperwork and eligibility requirements. The goal was lasting economic mobility, not simply a transaction.

What I owned

  • Intake and action plans: Opened participant files, collected income documentation, conducted introductory calls and established individualized next steps.
  • Financial preparation: Reviewed credit and spending, identified budget cuts, coached participants toward credit improvement and homeownership readiness, and prepared them for loan approval.
  • Purchase navigation: Educated families about vehicle options and worked directly with non-predatory lending partners and used-car dealerships through financing and purchase.
  • Charging access: Coordinated no-cost home charging support cases for eligible homeowners.
  • Performance monitoring: Tracked weekly cases, approvals, vehicle purchases, on-time and missed loan payments, loans fully repaid and participant testimonials.
  • Outreach and knowledge transfer: Tabled in the community; according to my firsthand account, GRID Alternatives staff shadowed my engagement work, interviewed me and reviewed operational numbers while developing related services.

Partnership ecosystem

The work took place in CHDC's clean-vehicle financing-assistance context, supported by the California Air Resources Board (CARB), and involved lending and dealership partners. Public documentation confirms the program context and a CHDC–GRID Alternatives clean-mobility relationship, but does not independently verify my personal counts or GRID's specific use of my methods.

Why it matters
