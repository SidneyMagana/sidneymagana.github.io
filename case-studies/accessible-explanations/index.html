Human-centered communication / Selected work

Make complexity
make sense.

Translating complicated ideas into explanations people can navigate—through attentive listening, clear structure, and visual communication.

Anthropology × Teaching coordination × Information design

01 / AUDIENCE NEED

Start with the person, not the information.

People approaching an unfamiliar subject need a clear starting point, relevant context, and a way to understand what comes next. My background in anthropology informs how I consider the audience's perspective rather than assuming shared knowledge.

In my community arts work with Sun Gallery, I coordinated and promoted free weekly family art classes connected to gallery exhibitions. The art teacher led instruction; my role supported access and communication around the program.

02 / COMMUNICATION CHALLENGE

Expert knowledge is not automatically accessible.

Complex ideas can become harder to follow when explanations rely on insider language, skip context, or present too many decisions at once.

In technical partner support at Google, I worked with knowledge resources and workflows where clarity mattered across functions. This reinforced the importance of documenting not only what to do, but the decision path behind it.

03 / TEACHING APPROACH

Listen. Organize. Explain.

My approach is to identify the audience and the question they are trying to answer, then organize information into a sequence that makes each step understandable. Listening and observation help me notice where assumptions may need to be explained.

My experience spans coordinating family arts programming and authoring or updating operational knowledge-base material. These are different settings, but both value audience awareness and clear communication.

05 / REFLECTION

Clarity is a design decision.

Anthropology encourages me to question assumptions. Listening helps me consider what an audience already knows. Information design gives those insights a visible structure.

Across teaching coordination and technical operations, I've learned to value explanations that respect people's time and make the next step easier to see. This case study describes my practice and rationale, not a measured improvement in comprehension.
