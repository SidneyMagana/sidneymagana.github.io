Doubling Package Processing Capacity Without Expanding the Floor

Creative spatial problem-solving during the COVID-19 surge

Amazon · Operations Program Manager · 2019–2020

01 / Case study

The challenge

Stay-at-home orders transformed package demand, while the station had finite floor space, equipment, and staffing. Our team needed to process more packages without simply doubling the physical footprint or workforce.

02 / Case study

Redesign the existing space

Rather than installing another conventional station, I reconsidered the floor plan: shelving, sorting positions, movement pathways, and transport carts. I repurposed carts for unconventional sorting functions and staged them for subsequent processing. This created additional capacity within the same space.

03 / Case study

Prepare the team for new risks

Anticipating an increase in hazardous items on the belt, I assigned specialized safety and task training to associates. During the period, our station also faced an active-shooter threat; a rapid lockdown prevented the threat from entering the station. I include this only as context for the safety-critical environment, without exposing security procedures.

04 / Case study

Observed result

Operational reports showed that the redesigned workflow supported approximately twice the package volume with the same floor area and staffing levels. I observed the figures during my employment, but the underlying reports are not publicly reproduced.

05 / Case study

Reflection

The breakthrough was not a new piece of equipment. It was questioning the assumed use of equipment we already had. Creative strategy can happen on a warehouse floor: the idea has to survive real-world constraints.

Independent retrospective. The approximately twofold processing increase is based on operational reporting observed during employment; original data is not reproduced. No confidential station diagrams or security protocols are included.
