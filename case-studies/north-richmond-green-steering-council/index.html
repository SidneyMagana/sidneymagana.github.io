Case study / Community program leadership / 2019

Building the infrastructure for community action.

Keeping a multi-stakeholder council working: facilitation, documentation, community communications, and continuity across neighborhood initiatives.

North Richmond Green Steering Council · Community Housing Development Corporation (CHDC)

View the impact panelExplore reconstructed newsletter

01 / The challenge

Many stakeholders. Shared priorities. One working forum.

North Richmond faced interconnected concerns including illegal dumping, neighborhood blight, environmental health and public-space maintenance. Progress required communication between residents, community organizations and public agencies.

The operational challenge: how do you keep a multi-stakeholder council organized, informed and consistently meeting?

My role: Council facilitator and operational lead

I was responsible for the council's meeting operations and communications. I facilitated sessions, prepared agendas, documented discussions and created the community newsletter. When presenting, I recorded proceedings to prepare notes afterward.

Scope: My firsthand account of the work I performed; meeting agendas independently document the recurring forum and topics discussed.

02 / Operating model

Structure that kept collaboration moving.

Facilitate

Led the council's meetings and structured the agenda around mitigation updates, agency reports, community-based project updates and open discussion.

Document

Prepared meeting materials, captured minutes, and recorded discussions when necessary to support accurate post-meeting notes.

Connect

Maintained a forum for city and county agencies, local nonprofits and community partners to share progress and raise priorities.

Communicate

Created a community newsletter to make neighborhood information more accessible beyond council meetings. Original copies are no longer available.

03 / CHDC-reported community activity

Neighborhood work at a meaningful scale.

CHDC also reported organizing the North Richmond Earth Day Festival. Separately, its Multicultural Family/Senior Center reported serving more than 5,000 residents through health and wellness, food security and workforce-development services. That center-wide figure is contextual, not a council outcome.

04 / Documentary record

What the surviving notes show.

November 4, 2019

Agency and neighborhood updates

Agenda topics included the developing North Richmond Blight and Mitigation Task Force, Las Deltas site updates, neighborhood gardens, senior-yard assistance and community resources.

December 4, 2019

Continuing coordination

Agenda topics included a Brookside Drive task force focus, county code enforcement representation, community cleanups, Urban Tilth events, a Watershed Project tree-planting report and food-distribution updates.

These are recorded agenda topics and partner reports, not claims that Sidney personally delivered each activity or that every planned activity was completed.

05 / Community communications

Making the work legible beyond the meeting.

I created a community newsletter as part of my council communications work. The original publication is no longer accessible, so the companion newsletter on this site is a modern editorial reconstruction using only topics present in the surviving November and December 2019 agendas.

Read the reconstructed newsletter

Why the distinction matters

The reconstruction demonstrates the editorial approach and communication function. It is not an original 2019 newsletter, and its layout and wording should not be treated as a historical facsimile.

06 / Reflection

Leadership is also the infrastructure.

Running the council reinforced that community initiatives need dependable systems: meetings that happen, agendas people can follow, notes that preserve context, and communications that extend information to a wider audience. This experience continues to inform how I approach program operations, cross-functional collaboration and human-centered strategy.
