A neighborhood is stronger when information moves between neighbors, organizations and public agencies.

Quarterly mailed newsletter · Reconstructed sample issue · Based on November–December 2019 meeting notes

From the council table

Working together on neighborhood priorities.

The North Richmond Green Steering Council's late-2019 agendas brought together updates on neighborhood mitigation, environmental health, code enforcement, community projects and local events. These recurring conversations offered partners a shared place to communicate what was underway and what needed attention.

A consistent meeting can be the beginning of consistent action.

Neighborhood watch / Project updates

Blight mitigation and cleanup.

The November 4 agenda noted that the North Richmond Blight and Mitigation Task Force was assembling, with a meeting planned for November 20. The December 4 agenda identified Brookside Drive as a focus for a December 10 task force meeting.

Updates also referenced Las Deltas sites and local cleanup activity. These were reports and plans discussed at the time, not independently confirmed project completions.

Greening / Community partners

Gardens, trees and volunteers.

The Watershed Project reported five gardens in its first cycle in November. In December, its update reported 50 trees planted and ongoing work on Hope Gardens and Block Ambassadors.

Urban Tilth's December update referenced Day of the Dead, Harvest Festival and Volunteer Day events, along with CSA program sign-ups.

Around the neighborhood

Support beyond beautification.

The November agenda included a proposed “Love Your Seniors” pilot to help seniors care for front yards. December partner updates included food distribution, a toy giveaway announcement and community-led cleanup reports.

Community communications

Stay connected.

The council agendas included time for agency reports, community-based project updates and open announcements. The reconstructed layout demonstrates how those updates could be translated into a readable quarterly publication for residents receiving it by mail.

Editor's note / Provenance

About this reconstruction.

Sidney Magaña recalls leading the council end to end and producing its quarterly community newsletter for distribution by postal mail. Original newsletter copies are unavailable. This edition was newly written and designed in 2026 to demonstrate that communications function, drawing only on topics documented in the November 4 and December 4, 2019 agendas.

Do not treat this as a historical original. Quotations, masthead, article wording and layout are newly created editorial elements. Project updates are described as meeting reports rather than outcomes attributable to Sidney.
