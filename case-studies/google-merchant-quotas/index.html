Technical project management / Workflow redesign

From Crisis
to Prevention.

Redesigning merchant quota management: from a hard-to-trace legacy process to a repeatable, proactive workflow with accountable human review.

Google · Technical Project Manager · 2022–2023

01 / Challenge

A merchant hit a limit. The original knowledge had moved on.

One of our early partners using the Google Shopping / Merchant Center integration reached a quota cap governing the number of products or advertisements it could publish. The resulting disruption exposed a difficult operational gap: the engineers and original team familiar with the integration were no longer on the team.

To investigate, I reverse-engineered the quota-management path and located a previous process owner. He had moved to another project and had limited capacity to support recurring requests. Relying on him each time was not a sustainable solution.

The opportunity: Create a process that would help partners get timely attention without repeatedly pulling a former subject matter expert away from his new work.

02 / Ownership

From finding the expert to becoming the owner.

I took ownership of reconstructing the approval process, learning the rules and decision criteria from the former subject matter expert, and creating a repeatable workflow that reduced dependence on his availability.

I determined whether merchants qualified for quota increases based on their activity and content quality. When an increase was not appropriate, I identified what the merchant needed to address before reconsideration.

As the process matured, I became the subject matter expert. When the former owner left the company, I assumed ongoing ownership of quota management for our Google Shopping partners.

This account distinguishes my workflow ownership and approval decisions from any underlying internal infrastructure or engineering work performed by others.

03 / Process redesign

From reactive support to proactive intervention.

Before / Merchant-initiated

  1. Merchant approaches or reaches applicable quota cap
  2. Merchant experiences publishing friction or disruption
  3. Partner contacts support
  4. Team traces the legacy approval path
  5. Quota increase reviewed and approved when appropriate

After / Proactive review

  1. Quota usage monitored through internal reporting
  2. Relevant conditions surface for attention
  3. Case created for review through the workflow
  4. I review merchant activity, legitimate product usage, and spam risk
  5. I approve an appropriate increase or explain required corrective actions
  6. Partner receives intervention without needing to initiate the same reactive support path

Simplified retrospective model. The workflow incorporated monitoring, alerts, and automated case creation; exact implementation details and proprietary thresholds are intentionally omitted. Quota increases remained subject to human approval.

04 / Design principles

Automate the signal. Keep the judgment.

Investigate: Recover undocumented knowledge, identify the correct authorization path, and clarify responsibility.

Monitor and route: Use reporting, alerts, and case creation to surface cases before merchants necessarily report problems.

Review: Check legitimate merchant activity and product quality, rather than approving every request automatically.

Explain: If a merchant does not qualify, specify what must change before a future increase can be considered.

Document: Create guidance that allows the process to continue beyond any one individual's institutional knowledge.

Observed operational outcomes

Counts are firsthand retrospective estimates. Outcomes are qualitative observations, not independently measured reductions, guaranteed prevention, or claims that all merchant quota caps were removed.

06 / Reflection

Good support can happen before the problem is visible.

The original incident was a support problem. The deeper challenge was ownership: knowing who could make a decision, under which conditions, and how to keep that knowledge available when teams changed.

By rebuilding the process and eventually owning it, I learned that effective automation is not about removing human decisions. It is about making the right decisions possible at the right moment.
