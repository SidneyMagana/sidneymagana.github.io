Service design · Housing operations · Resident experience

From Two Years Behind to Two Months Ahead

Redesigning affordable housing operations around the people who use them

Volunteers of America · Lord Tennyson Apartments, Hayward

01 / Case study

The challenge

Resident recertifications were approximately two years behind schedule. Staff turnover had left inconsistent administrative procedures, and residents often relied on in-person visits to request maintenance or coordinate paperwork. I saw an opportunity to redesign the experience for residents, office employees, and maintenance staff.

02 / Case study

Build relationships and a repeatable process

I introduced myself to families and established a predictable appointment schedule. I mailed paper notices about a month ahead, allowed a week for rescheduling requests, and invited residents to submit documents early. In-person appointments could then focus on confirmation and signatures, generally taking 45 minutes or less. I rewrote and standardized forms and trained colleagues to follow a consistent process.

03 / Case study

Clear the backlog within approved requirements

To address missing historical recertifications, I used a formally approved streamlined review process based on prior-year tax records rather than reconstructing every historical income source. This helped reduce repetitive work while operating within the approved approach.

04 / Case study

Connect digital intake to hands-on maintenance

Previously, many residents submitted work orders face-to-face instead of through their online portal. I made online submissions a usable anytime option. Each morning, the office printed requests, assigned them by urgency, and gave them to maintenance staff. Team members returned signed updates indicating whether they visited and why any work remained incomplete. The office retained digital tracking and a clearer view of outstanding requests.

05 / Case study

Use work-order data to spot patterns

More consistent records made it easier to identify repeated concerns—such as pests and plumbing problems—and see which buildings might need larger projects. The goal was not only to close individual requests but to understand conditions across the property.

06 / Case study

Results and reflection

Over approximately eight months, I helped move the recertification schedule from about two years behind to two months ahead. Appointments generally took no more than 45 minutes. I observed improved work-order visibility, shorter turnaround, happier residents, and increased participation in recurring holiday events. Exact maintenance response-time improvements were not independently measured. The lesson: better systems make more room for human connection.

Independent retrospective based on firsthand experience. The streamlined recertification method was formally approved according to my account; original compliance records and performance reports are not reproduced.
